Robust Model Organisms for Sandbagging

Abstract

Malicious model developers can deliberately modify language models to strategically underperform on dangerous tasks in order to evade regulations, a behavior known as sandbagging. Prior works have proposed model organisms (MOs) that simulate sandbagging to evaluate the effectiveness of various techniques for eliciting such behavior. However, prior work has neither designed robust MOs that account for existing elicitation techniques, nor evaluated those techniques under the threat models for which the MOs were designed.

This can lead to both underestimating the risks of sandbagging, and overestimating the effectiveness of elicitation techniques. Thus, systematically designing and evaluating robust MOs under appropriate threat models remains an open problem.

We address this gap by framing the problem as a security game between an auditor and a malicious model developer, and identify three key requirements for MOs: (i) effectiveness (sandbagging of intended capabilities), (ii) utility-preservation (maintaining utility on standard capabilities), and (iii) robustness (resistance to elicitation techniques).

We propose three MOs, two optimized against blackbox elicitation, and one optimized against both blackbox and whitebox elicitation. Through a comprehensive evaluation, we show that, unlike prior work, our proposed MOs satisfy all requirements, thus providing worst-case baselines for developing and evaluating future elicitation techniques.
